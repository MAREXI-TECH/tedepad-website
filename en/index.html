Anisakis

The Problem

Anisakiasis is a parasitic disease caused by the accidental ingestion of fish contaminated by larvae of worms from the Anisakidae family. Added to this problem are the allergic reactions (some severe) caused by proteins specific to Anisakis, even when the fish is dead.

The life cycle of Anisakis species takes place in the marine environment, where they pass through several hosts throughout their lives: crustaceans, fish, cephalopods, and marine mammals.

The treatment of contaminated viscera and waste returned to the sea during on-board fish processing is essential to reduce the concentration of these parasites in commercially valuable species.

TEDEPAD®

The Solution

It directly intervenes in the marine ecosystem, minimizing the problem of parasites in fish at its source.

Technology and Validated Product

The TEDEPAD has been validated within the framework of the PARASITE Project  http://parasite-project.eu/project with partners from 13 countries and funded by the European Union.

In 2024, within the framework of the GESTPAR Management Commission from the General Secretariat of Fisheries (MAPA) to the Marine Research Institute (IIM) - CSIC, the latest TEDEPAD model was revalidated after demonstrating its effectiveness in inactivating parasites on board. BOE-A-2022-24655.pdf

Automatic Operation

The TEDEPAD® is fully automatic and requires no consumables. Its installation on the vessel requires no major modifications, as it is inserted directly into the existing waste extraction pipes in the fishing ground. It does not interfere with daily work on board.
